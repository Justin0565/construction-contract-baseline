# Tag Team Manual v1.0

## Legal Effect Tag Dictionary and Tagging Rules

## 法律效果标签手册

This file is the governing project-control dictionary and methodology for legal-effect tags.

Use this manual together with `project-control/01_Dashboard_Classification_Methodology_Memo.md`, `project-control/07_ELEMENT_METHODOLOGY_MANUAL.md` and `project-control/09_SCOPE_BASED_DASHBOARD_STANDARD.md`.

---

## 1. Tag positioning

Clause = source contract text.

Path = the functional location of the issue.

Element = the smallest functional contract mechanism inside a clause.

Tag = the legal effect created by an element.

Checklist = the use of elements and tags to review another contract.

Tags are applied to clause elements, not to the whole clause by default.

The functional path identifies where the issue belongs. The element identifies the operative mechanism. The tag records only the legal effect expressly created or regulated by that element.

---

## 2. General tagging rules

1. Do not tag by legal intuition.
2. Do not tag merely because an issue may arise in practice.
3. Apply a tag only where the source wording satisfies the applicable definition in this manual.
4. Record the source clause, relevant element, express wording and tag reason for every applied tag.
5. Tags attach to elements, not to an entire clause by default.
6. Do not create, infer, rename, merge, broaden or apply tags outside the approved dictionary unless expressly instructed.
7. Do not use a generic `EOT` tag.
8. Do not use generic `Claim` as a standalone legal-effect tag unless expressly approved. Use `Claim for EOT` and `Claim for Cost` instead.
9. One element may receive more than one approved tag only where the express wording independently satisfies each tag definition.
10. Similar commercial outcomes do not make distinct tags interchangeable.

## 4. Tag dictionary

### 4.1 Claim for EOT

Applies where the clause expressly creates or regulates a Contractor’s claim for EOT, extension of time, extension of the Time for Completion, extended time for completion, or equivalent time relief.

Do not apply merely because the clause affects programme, progress, sequence, timing, or completion.

### 4.2 Claim for Cost

Applies where the clause expressly creates or regulates a Contractor’s claim for additional payment, Cost, costs, Cost Plus Profit, additional costs, expense, loss and expense, or other monetary relief, whether profit is included or excluded.

May include variation-related claims depending on the contract wording.

For FIDIC 2017 Red Book, ordinary Variation adjustment under 13.3.1 is not treated as a Contractor Claim under 20.2 unless the clause wording expressly says so.

### 4.3 Contractor Breach / Default

Applies where the clause expressly refers to the Contractor’s fail, failure, default, failure to perform, failure to comply, delay in performing, or equivalent non-performance wording.

Do not apply merely because a clause imposes a Contractor obligation.

### 4.4 Employer Breach / Default

Applies where the clause expressly refers to the Employer’s fail, failure, default, failure to perform, failure to comply, delay in performing, or equivalent non-performance wording.

Do not apply merely because a clause imposes an Employer obligation.

### 4.5 Determination

Applies where the clause expressly requires or permits the Engineer, Employer’s Representative, certifier, DAAB, or other decision-maker to agree, determine, decide, certify, assess, or fix a matter under a contractual decision mechanism.

### 4.6 Condition Precedent

Applies where a notice, submission, approval, consent, time limit, certificate, guarantee, or other step is expressly made a precondition to entitlement, payment, claim, commencement, action, or contractual relief.

### 4.7 Time Bar

Applies where failure to comply with a time limit expressly causes loss of entitlement, deemed waiver, preclusion, finality, inadmissibility, or another bar to claim or relief.

### 4.8 Deemed Approval

Applies where silence, non-response, failure to object, or expiry of time is expressly deemed to be approval, consent, no-objection, acceptance, or agreement.

### 4.9 Deemed Rejection

Applies where silence, non-response, failure to decide, or expiry of time is expressly deemed to be rejection, disagreement, dispute, refusal, or non-acceptance.

### 4.10 Deduction

Applies only where the Employer is expressly entitled to deduct an amount from sums due to the Contractor, the Contract Price, an IPC, Final Payment, or other payment otherwise payable to the Contractor.

Deduction applies to the Employer only.

### 4.11 Withholding

Applies where either party is expressly entitled to withhold payment, certification, release, performance, approval, consent, or another contractual step.

Withholding may apply to either Contractor or Employer.

### 4.12 Set-off

Applies where either party is expressly entitled to set off one amount, debt, liability, claim, or entitlement against another.

Set-off may apply to either Contractor or Employer.

### 4.13 Indemnity

Applies where either party expressly indemnifies, holds harmless, or protects the other party against loss, liability, damage, claim, cost, charge, or expense.

Indemnity may apply to either Contractor or Employer.

Do not infer indemnity from general risk allocation.

### 4.14 Remedy

Applies where the clause expressly provides for correction, repair, replacement, rejection, remedying defects, remedial work, re-performance, making good, or another curative measure.

### 4.15 Termination Trigger

Applies where the clause expressly identifies an event, default, failure, insolvency, prolonged suspension, non-payment, corruption, abandonment, or other circumstance giving a party the right to terminate.

### 4.16 Back-to-back

Applies where the clause expressly requires obligations, liabilities, standards, conditions, procedures, rights, risks, or indemnities under one contract to be incorporated into or mirrored in another contract, usually a subcontract.

### 4.17 Waiver / Non-Waiver / Discharge

Applies where the clause expressly provides for waiver, deemed waiver, non-waiver, discharge, release, final settlement, loss of entitlement, or states that a particular act, omission, approval, payment, certificate, instruction, consent, no-objection, or failure to object shall or shall not constitute a waiver.

This tag includes clauses marked as not being considered a waiver.

### 4.18 Counterclaim / Countercharge

Applies where the clause expressly creates or regulates an Employer claim, counterclaim, countercharge, or monetary claim against the Contractor, including claims for payment, damages, reduction, compensation, or recovery.

Do not apply merely because the Employer has a deduction, withholding, set-off, or valuation reduction right.

## 5. Important distinctions

- Claim for Cost is not ordinary payment.
- Claim for EOT is not programme adjustment.
- Deduction is not Withholding.
- Withholding is not Set-off.
- Contractor Breach / Default is not any Contractor obligation.
- Employer Breach / Default is not any Employer obligation.
- Indemnity is not general risk allocation.
- Waiver / Non-Waiver / Discharge includes both waiver and express non-waiver wording.
- Variation may be a claim in some contracts, but ordinary FIDIC 2017 Red Book 13.3.1 Variation adjustment is not a Contractor Claim under 20.2.

## 6. Tagging QC rules

Before approving an applied tag, confirm all of the following:

1. The source clause and exact clause number are identified.
2. The tag is attached to a defined clause element.
3. The tag name exactly matches an approved tag in section 3.
4. The express source wording satisfies the complete definition in section 4.
5. The tag reason quotes or precisely identifies the operative wording without relying on legal intuition.
6. The tag is not being applied merely because the issue could arise in practice.
7. Party-specific tags identify the correct party.
8. Claim for EOT and Claim for Cost are assessed separately.
9. Deduction, Withholding and Set-off are assessed separately.
10. Any apparent overlap with another tag is documented and each tag is independently supported.
11. Any uncertainty is reported for legal review; it is not resolved by broadening the tag definition.
12. Existing tag mappings are not automatically converted solely because this dictionary is adopted.

Recommended QC record:

```text
Clause reference:
Element name:
Element type:
Approved tag:
Express wording anchor:
Tag reason:
Party affected:
QC status:
Reviewer:
```

## 7. Prohibited practices

Codex and project contributors must not:

- create a new tag without express instruction;
- infer a tag from commercial context or legal intuition;
- rename an approved tag;
- merge two approved tags;
- broaden a definition to capture wording it does not expressly cover;
- apply a tag merely because the issue may occur in practice;
- apply tags to a whole clause by default;
- use generic `EOT` as an approved tag;
- use generic `Claim` as a standalone legal-effect tag unless expressly approved;
- use undifferentiated `Breach / Default` instead of the party-specific approved tags;
- treat ordinary payment as Claim for Cost;
- treat programme adjustment as Claim for EOT;
- treat Deduction, Withholding and Set-off as interchangeable;
- treat a Contractor or Employer obligation as a breach/default without express non-performance wording;
- infer Indemnity from general risk allocation;
- treat every Variation adjustment as a Contractor claim;
- alter existing tag mappings automatically when applying this manual.
